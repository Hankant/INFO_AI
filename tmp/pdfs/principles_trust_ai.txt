Abstract                                                                                                                Sections


Artificial intelligence (AI) increasingly performs tasks once reserved                                                  Introduction

for humans, raising questions about when, why and how people                                                            Defining ‘trust in AI’
trust machines — and whether they should do so in the first place.                                                      Principles for understanding
In this Review, we identify six principles that help to structure an                                                    trust in AI
understanding of trust in AI and highlight its socially embedded                                                        Ethical considerations
nature: that trust in AI is inferred; that trustworthiness, trust and
                                                                                                                        Summary and future
trusting behaviour are distinct; that trust in AI is about both morality                                                directions
and performance; and that trust in AI is agent-specific; individually
variable; and strategically motivated. The inferred, multidimensional,
dynamic and contextual nature of trust in AI illustrates that ‘trust in
AI’ is not one thing, but varies across different systems, individuals
and contexts. We end by considering broader ethical implications of
studying trust in AI and argue that trust in AI requires both studying
how people think and reflecting on the kind of world that trust in AI
serves to create.
